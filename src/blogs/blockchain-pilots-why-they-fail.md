Recently, a Deloitte survey found that while 84% of global executives believed blockchain would eventually achieve mainstream adoption, only 23% had actually initiated a live deployment. This disconnect is not lost on CTOs, CPOs, and transformation leaders who have watched countless pilots stall before reaching production.

Blockchain, heralded as the answer to trustless collaboration, has often been approached by enterprises as a silver bullet for proprietary data sharing. Yet, the reality is more nuanced. Most pilots falter not due to technology, but because of misaligned incentives, unclear governance, and a lack of integration with existing business processes. Too often, pilots are launched in isolation—driven by innovation teams rather than embedded in the core value chain. The result? Impressive proofs of concept that never scale beyond the sandbox.

For CXOs, the lesson is clear: success with blockchain requires more than technical validation. It demands executive sponsorship, cross-functional buy-in, and a relentless focus on business outcomes. Governance frameworks must be established early, and incentives for all ecosystem participants must be aligned from day one. Only then can pilots transition from isolated experiments to production-grade platforms that deliver real enterprise value.

As the hype cycle matures, the question for executive teams is not whether blockchain can work, but whether your organization is prepared to operationalize it at scale. What hidden frictions or misaligned incentives might be undermining your next pilot—before it even begins?
