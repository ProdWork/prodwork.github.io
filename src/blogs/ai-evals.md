Recently, Gartner reported that 85% of AI projects fail to deliver business value, often due to a lack of robust evaluation frameworks. As C-suite leaders, we must recognize that intuition and anecdotal wins are not proxies for measurable progress. Two weeks before this post, the industry was abuzz with stories of AI models quietly regressing after seemingly minor updates—yet most organizations lacked the instrumentation to notice until it was too late.

Evals—structured, repeatable evaluations—are the missing link between AI ambition and operational excellence. Rather than relying on gut feel or isolated success stories, evals provide a disciplined, data-driven approach to tracking model performance. The process is straightforward: select real-world inputs, define what “good” means for your business, and score outputs against those criteria. Whether you’re measuring accuracy, compliance, or user experience, the goal is to create a shared language for quality that transcends technical silos.

For senior product leaders, the imperative is clear: embed evals into your release pipeline. This isn’t about academic rigor or perfection—it’s about creating a safety net that surfaces regressions before they impact customers or revenue. Start small, iterate, and ensure every stakeholder—engineering, product, and executive—can interpret the results. Over time, your evals will evolve, capturing new edge cases and aligning with shifting business priorities.

The real question for the C-suite isn’t whether your AI is improving, but whether you can prove it—consistently, transparently, and at scale. As the market accelerates, will your organization be able to separate signal from noise, or will you be left reacting to problems you never saw coming?
