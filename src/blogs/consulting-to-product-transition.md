When a senior consultant leaves the boardroom for a product leadership role, the real shift isn’t just in title—it’s in accountability. I remember a former colleague’s last day at a top consulting firm: the celebration was brief, but the reality of owning outcomes set in quickly. For Chief Product Officers and CXOs, the difference is stark. Consulting rewards insight and persuasion; product leadership demands endurance, judgment, and the willingness to live with every decision.

In consulting, the work ends with a recommendation. In product, that’s where it begins. Early trade-offs—scope, architecture, governance—echo for years. There are no clean resets, only compounding consequences. The transition forces leaders to embrace uncertainty, make calls with imperfect data, and stay accountable as reality unfolds.

For executives making this leap, the question is not whether you can advise, but whether you can deliver—again and again, as the stakes rise. What would it take for your leadership to thrive when the safety net of consulting is gone?
